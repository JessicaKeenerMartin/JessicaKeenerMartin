# Program Operations Readiness Checklist

## Daily readiness

### Staffing and communication

- Confirm coverage for every shift and identify the shift lead.
- Review call-outs, schedule changes, transportation needs, and high-priority handoffs.
- Verify that staff know how to reach clinical, medical, and leadership support.
- Assign responsibility for open operational issues.

### Facility and safety

- Walk client and staff areas for hazards, cleanliness, access issues, and needed repairs.
- Confirm emergency exits, fire equipment, first-aid supplies, and required postings are accessible.
- Check controlled-access areas and follow medication-storage procedures.
- Document concerns and assign a completion date.

### Client flow

- Reconcile the census, expected admissions, discharges, appointments, and housing assignments.
- Confirm transportation plans and driver availability.
- Review special accommodations without sharing unnecessary protected information.
- Make sure schedule changes are communicated consistently.

## Weekly readiness

- Audit a sample of operational documentation for completeness.
- Review staff credentials, training deadlines, and expiring certifications.
- Check supply levels and place orders before shortages affect services.
- Review vehicle condition, maintenance needs, and required documentation.
- Follow up on open vendor, facility, and technology requests.
- Look for repeated problems that require a process change rather than another reminder.

## Monthly leadership review

- Analyze incident patterns, overtime, call-outs, admission delays, maintenance trends, and training completion.
- Confirm policies still match actual practice.
- Test emergency contact lists and escalation procedures.
- Recognize strong performance and provide direct, documented coaching where needed.
- Select a small number of measurable priorities for the next month.

## Operating principles

1. Assign ownership—an unassigned task is usually an unfinished task.
2. Communicate once, clearly, through the correct channel.
3. Fix systems when the same mistake keeps happening.
4. Protect client dignity and confidentiality in every operational decision.
5. Document enough for the next person to understand what happened and what comes next.
