# Emergency Continuity Planning Framework

## Objective

Maintain safe, humane, and organized behavioral health services during severe weather, utility loss, transportation disruption, staffing shortages, or facility emergencies.

## Before an emergency

- Maintain current staff, leadership, vendor, utility, pharmacy, transportation, and emergency-service contacts.
- Identify essential services that must continue and those that may be temporarily modified.
- Define decision authority and backup leadership.
- Keep emergency supplies appropriate to the program’s population and facility.
- Plan for medication access, refrigeration needs, charging, sanitation, food, water, and client communication.
- Identify alternate programming spaces, transportation options, and evacuation destinations.
- Practice concise shift-to-shift emergency handoffs.

## During an emergency

### First priorities

1. Account for every client and staff member.
2. Address immediate medical, psychiatric, environmental, and security risks.
3. Stabilize essential medications, food, water, shelter, sanitation, and communication.
4. Establish one reliable source of operational updates.
5. Document major decisions, incidents, expenditures, and unmet needs.

### Communication rhythm

- Set regular update times so staff are not working from rumors or outdated information.
- Communicate what is known, what is not yet known, who is deciding, and when the next update will occur.
- Give clients honest, calm explanations and preserve as much routine as conditions allow.
- Notify external partners only through authorized channels.

### Continuity decisions

- Adapt programming while preserving clinically meaningful structure.
- Combine duties carefully without abandoning supervision or safety requirements.
- Monitor staff fatigue and arrange relief before judgment and performance deteriorate.
- Escalate early when supplies, staffing, facility conditions, or medication access approach unsafe levels.

## Recovery after the event

- Confirm normal operations deliberately rather than assuming the crisis has ended.
- Reconcile medications, supplies, census records, transportation, staffing, and outstanding incidents.
- Debrief with staff and invite specific client feedback.
- Record what worked, what failed, and which relationships or resources made the greatest difference.
- Assign owners and deadlines to corrective actions.
- Update the written continuity plan while the experience is still fresh.

## Leadership standard

Emergency leadership is steady, visible, honest, and practical. The goal is not to pretend conditions are normal; it is to create enough clarity and structure for people to remain safe and supported until normal operations return.
