# Behavioral Health Admissions Workflow

## Purpose

A simple, repeatable framework for moving a prospective client from first contact to an appropriate admission decision while protecting safety, privacy, and continuity of care.

## 1. Initial inquiry

- Record the caller’s name, contact preference, referral source, and immediate concern.
- Identify whether the caller is the prospective client, a family member, a provider, or another authorized party.
- Screen immediately for urgent medical or psychiatric danger and follow the organization’s emergency escalation policy when necessary.
- Explain the admissions process in plain language and set expectations for next steps.

## 2. Pre-admission assessment

- Collect current substance-use history, withdrawal risk, medical conditions, medications, psychiatric history, functional needs, and recent treatment history.
- Confirm identity and insurance information using approved secure systems.
- Obtain releases of information before communicating protected information to outside parties.
- Document objective facts and distinguish them from unverified reports.

## 3. Clinical and medical review

- Route the completed assessment and relevant records to the designated clinician or medical decision-maker.
- Clearly flag time-sensitive safety concerns, medication needs, mobility limitations, and potential barriers to placement.
- Track outstanding records and follow up without allowing the referral to disappear from the queue.
- Never promise admission before the authorized reviewer confirms appropriateness.

## 4. Benefits, availability, and placement

- Verify benefits or coordinate with the appropriate financial team.
- Match the approved level of care with current program and housing capacity.
- Maintain an accurate bed board and document anticipated discharges carefully.
- Provide transparent alternatives when the requested placement is unavailable or inappropriate.

## 5. Admission coordination

- Confirm arrival time, transportation, required documents, permitted belongings, medication procedures, and the receiving staff member.
- Give the client and family a single clear point of contact.
- Notify clinical, nursing, operations, and housing staff using the approved internal handoff process.
- Prepare the chart and intake materials before arrival whenever possible.

## 6. Arrival and handoff

- Welcome the client respectfully and protect privacy during check-in.
- Complete required safety checks, consents, releases, medication reconciliation, and property procedures.
- Communicate unresolved concerns directly to the receiving team.
- Confirm that the client understands the immediate schedule and whom to ask for help.

## Quality checks

- Every referral has an owner and a next action.
- Safety concerns are escalated, not buried in notes.
- Admission decisions are made only by authorized staff.
- Capacity information is current.
- Protected information is shared only through approved channels.
- Declined or deferred referrals receive a documented disposition and appropriate resources.
